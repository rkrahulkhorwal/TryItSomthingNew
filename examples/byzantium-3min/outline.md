## 4. Re-Hook

**Twist:** The first and last emperors were both **Constantine, son of Helena**.

**Placement:** **~2:10 (130 s, ≈72 % of runtime)**, inside Chapter 5. It comes after the overland-ships beat and the 7,000 vs 80,000 counter, just before the final assault.

**Draft line (~25 words):**
> "The man defending those walls was named Constantine. His mother's name was Helena. Exactly like the man who founded the city, 1,123 years earlier."

**Visual:** split screen. Left: Constantine I and Helena, 330. Right: Constantine XI and Helena Dragaš, 1453. The two dates slide together and the "circle closing" sting plays.

**Why here:** Viewers already know the city will fall, so this is the moment they are most likely to leave. The twist turns a predictable ending into a story that loops back on itself, and it carries them through the final 50 seconds.

**Final line (~20 words):**

This closes the hook's question: who was ruling the Roman Empire in 1453? The Romans were.

### Visual metaphors
- **The walls as a shell:** introduced in Ch. 2 as an unbreakable egg-like shell around the city. Cracks appear only from inside (riot) or from the sea side (1204) until Ch. 5, when gunpowder finally shatters the shell from outside.
- **The gold coin as the empire's heartbeat** (Ch. 4): bright and steady for centuries, then dimming before Manzikert. This foreshadows the decline.
- **The stamped label** (motif): the empire's identity is literally overwritten by a printing press after its death.

### Hedging rules for VO and on-screen text
- Theodora's speech: **paraphrase only**, attributed to Procopius.
- Nika toll, Basil's blinding, plague toll: **"according to…" / "historians argue"** on both the VO and the card.
- 670s siege: "Arab fleets **pressed** the city for years," not "a five-year siege."
- Orban: "**reportedly** offered his guns to the Byzantines first."
- Constantine XI: "body **never certainly identified**." Avoid the regalia-stripping legend and the Kerkoporta gate.
- Wolf: "**popularised**," not "invented."
- Cyrillic: "developed by **disciples of** Cyril and Methodius."
